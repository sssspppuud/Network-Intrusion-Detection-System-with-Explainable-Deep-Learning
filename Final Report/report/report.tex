\section{Introduction, Motivation, and Aims}
\subsection{Motivation}
The use of smart and connected medical devices has rapidly made it's way into
the delivery of various healthcare services, leading to the rapid expansion of the
Internet of Medical Things (IoMT). This subset of the Internet of Things (IoT)
includes many medical devices such as remote heart rate monitors, temperature
trackers, and continuous glucose monitors, which can all be connected and used by
healthcare professionals to keep track of patients health and wellbeing.
Using IoMT devices allows healthcare professionals to continuously monitor patients
to make more informed decisions for diagnosis and treatment plans. For example,
data derived from IoMT devices was successfully used to predict the risk
patients have for developing heart disease \cite{baseer2024healthcare}.
However, alongside the rapid integration of IoMT technology into healthcare delivery,
the attack surface of healthcare infrastructure to also significantly expanded. By the
end of 2025, there had been 710 recorded healthcare data breaches, each of which
affecting over 500 people \cite{hipaajournal}. These breaches impacted millions of
patients, exposing sensitive medical records and other user data.
\newline
Within this rapidly evolving landscape, new vulnerabilities and cyber attacks
become major threats toward IoMT networks. Defenses such as firewalls alone
are no longer enough to ensure the security of these networks, requiring the use of
an Intrusion Detection System (IDS) to flag malicious network activity missed by the
firewall.

Traditional, signature-based IDSs often fail to detect more modern, unknown attack
signatures, leading to Machine Learning (ML) based IDSs becoming a key area of recent
research in network intrusion detection. ML based IDSs are able to recognise the
complex patterns found in network traffic to identify malicious activity, instead of
simple rule based systems. However, the lack of interpretability of these ML based
systems limits the widespread adoption, especially in healthcare setting where the
safety and privacy of both patients and staff are critical. To address this lack of
interpretability in more complex models, this project focuses on this critical gap by
developing a Deep Neural Network (DNN) IDS, and uses Explainable Artificial
Intelligence (XAI) techniques to verify the performance and accuracy of the model.
